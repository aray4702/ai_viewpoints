# The idea

**Their Take: what people think, with sources.**

## The situation

Leaders and thinkers in AI, technology, investing, business, and politics share what they
think in public, constantly and for free:

- **Many formats.** Posts on X and Reddit, YouTube talks, podcast interviews, conference panels,
  blog posts and newsletters, books, and research papers.
- **Many voices.** Beyond the famous names, niche experts (a semiconductor analyst, a
  biotech investor, a policy researcher) share deep, specialized views with smaller audiences.
- **Growing volume.** A single person may publish across five platforms, and long-form media
  keeps getting longer: two- and three-hour podcasts are now normal.

These views are valuable. A well-reasoned prediction from someone with inside knowledge can
change what you build, invest in, or learn. That value is the reason people follow these voices
at all.

## The issue

The value is real, but almost no one can get at it efficiently:

- **The views are buried.** The one sharp, contrarian prediction sits 90 minutes into a
  three-hour podcast, or in the middle of a 10,000-word essay. Most of the surrounding content is
  context, small talk, or things you already know.
- **The views are scattered.** Keeping up with one person means following them on every
  platform they use. Keeping up with a topic, say semiconductors, means following dozens of
  people across all of those platforms.
- **Finding and consuming media takes hours.** You first have to discover that a relevant
  interview or post exists, then watch, listen to, or read all of it, to extract a handful of
  ideas.
- **Summaries don't fix it.** Generic summaries flatten everything to the same level of
  importance, lose who said what (especially in interviews, where the guest's views are often
  the point), and rarely let you check the original words.
- **Valuable views get lost.** Because of all this, most people see only the views that happen
  to go viral. Insights from niche experts, and from guests on shows you don't follow, never
  reach the people who would act on them.

The result: people who depend on knowing what experts think, such as investors, builders,
researchers, and policy watchers, either spend hours each week consuming media or miss views that
matter.

## The idea

Build a system that watches the internet for new media from people worth listening to, and mines
it for **viewpoints**: distinct opinions, predictions, judgments, or recommendations, each with
the exact quote and a link back to the source.

The viewpoints are tagged, stored permanently, published on a website and in Discord, and
delivered to people who subscribe with filters such as `karpathy`, `andrew ng`, or
`stock & semiconductor`.

## What makes a viewpoint worth keeping

A viewpoint has to be:

1. **Novel**: a non-obvious, contrarian, or insider take, or a specific prediction, not
   conventional wisdom.
2. **Useful**: specific enough to change how you think, with reasoning, numbers, or examples
   behind it.
3. **Practical**: something you could act on: what to build, invest in, learn, use, or watch for.

Each piece of media yields **at most three** viewpoints. The goal is the best few ideas, not a
summary of everything that was said.

## Credit goes to whoever said it

In an interview, the guest often has the most valuable views. Their Take credits each viewpoint
to the person who actually holds it. When a tracked podcast host interviews someone new, that
guest is added automatically and their viewpoints appear under their own name, marked "via" the
host's show.

## Trust

Every viewpoint carries the speaker's exact words and a link to the source, deep-linked to the
timestamp for videos. Quotes are checked against the source text, and any viewpoint whose quote
can't be found is discarded. Readers can always verify a take in one click.
